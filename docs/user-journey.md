# User journey

## Context

Eleanor, 78, returns home with moderate dementia, diabetes, heart failure, mobility limitations, medication changes, wound care, monitoring, and follow-ups. Her daughter Maya works full time, lives 30 minutes away, has 20 caregiving hours per week, and wants to approve external messages.

## Journey

1. Maya opens the resettable synthetic mission and sees the safety boundary.
2. The Care Inbox shows ten dated sources, review states, and extracted responsibility counts.
3. The compiler presents 26 Care Commitment Objects with exact source references.
4. Maya corrects an uncertain voice-note interpretation without erasing the original.
5. The Reality Check declares `NOT EXECUTABLE` and identifies isolated, actionable blockers.
6. The medication workflow blocks conflicting insulin instructions and prepares a pharmacist question.
7. A controlled professional response is recorded; Maya explicitly confirms activation.
8. Daniel accepts transportation using task-specific access that withholds diagnosis and medication data.
9. Equipment and wound-care capability are assigned through controlled community and home-health simulations.
10. Evening work is redistributed until Maya has an explainable capacity margin.
11. Provider acknowledgement is tracked to an outcome.
12. The unknown “blue pill” instruction remains abstained and professionally escalated.
13. The mission reaches `READY WITH CONTROLS`, not an unconditional Ready state.
14. Accountability Receipts, permission history, and validation evidence remain inspectable.

## Cognitive-load controls

- One primary action at a time on Today’s Mission.
- Nonurgent updates are grouped.
- Color is never the sole status signal.
- Plain-language descriptions accompany compiler codes.
- Interrupted work is preserved in explicit lifecycle states.
- Print output is available for the daily mission.
