# Product vision

Healthcare gives families instructions, but no system owns the space between those instructions and real life.

HEARTH is a caregiver-first execution-assurance layer for the first 30 days after discharge. It converts fragmented instructions into a care mission where every responsibility has an owner, deadline, source, confidence, permission, dependency, safety boundary, completion test, backup, and human escalation path.

## Product thesis

The differentiating unit is the Care Commitment Object, not a summary or reminder. The system asks:

1. What exactly must happen?
2. Who is permitted and able to do it?
3. Is the full plan realistic and safe?
4. What evidence establishes completion?
5. What happens when information or execution fails?

## Primary user and outcome

The primary user is a family caregiver supporting an older adult with dementia and multiple chronic conditions at home. HEARTH should reduce invisible coordination work without moving it into a new homework-heavy dashboard. Its success is more time and attention available for human care.

## Design principles

- Care-recipient dignity and choice are first-class constraints.
- Moderate dementia does not automatically remove decision authority.
- AI interpretations never silently become verified clinical facts.
- Human and professional authority are visible, not buried in policy text.
- Unaffected work may continue while a high-risk item is blocked.
- Completion is an outcome, not a generated message.
- External action defaults to **Prepare**.
- The product states uncertainty and abstains before it improvises.
- Synthetic, low-cost web access is the Phase 1 default; no proprietary hardware is required.
