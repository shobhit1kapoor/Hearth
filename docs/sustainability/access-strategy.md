# Access strategy

## Design commitments

- No mandatory proprietary hardware. HEARTH is designed for a standards-based browser on an existing phone, tablet, or computer.
- Essential household coordination should have a free or low-cost access path during an approved pilot.
- Caregiver access should not require selling personal health information or targeted advertising.
- Organizations may sponsor access, but sponsorship must not give the organization control over a care recipient's permissions or private caregiver notes.
- Phone, printable, and human-support alternatives remain necessary for households affected by disability, language, connectivity, or device barriers.

## Proposed pathways

1. **Household essential tier:** source review, commitments, permission controls, receipts, export, and deletion at no or low cost.
2. **Organization-sponsored tier:** aging, caregiver-support, health-plan, health-system, employer, or public-program funding for onboarding, support, and approved integrations.
3. **Supported pilot access:** grant or prize funding covers participant compensation, accessibility accommodation, technical support, and evaluation.

No payer, employer, health system, public agency, or community organization has committed to reimbursement or sponsorship. Phase 2 must test willingness to sponsor without making essential access conditional on a specific organization.
